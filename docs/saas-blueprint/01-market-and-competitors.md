The 70.62% independent share is the whole thesis. Chains are already served — Orda has KFC Nigeria
and Eat'N'Go. Independents are not, and they are the majority of the market and the fastest segment
to reach because the buying committee is one person.

The market has finished buying order-taking. It is now buying decisions. Any platform launching in
2026 that treats reporting as a bolt-on is launching into the slowest-growing part of its own
category.

## 1.2 Competitor analysis

Eighteen platforms across four groups. Prices are as published in mid-to-late 2026.

### Group A — US full-stack POS

#### Toast

- **Purpose:** end-to-end restaurant POS, payments, payroll, marketing. Restaurants only.
- **Customers:** US full-service and QSR, single site to enterprise.
- **Pricing:** Starter Kit $0/mo (up to 2 terminals); Point of Sale $69/mo; Build Your Own quoted.
  Processing 2.49% + $0.15 standard, 2.99% + $0.15 on the pay-as-you-go plan. Hardware ~$1,024
  one-time. Add-ons: online ordering ~$75, loyalty $50, gift cards $50, email marketing $75, KDS
  $25, payroll $90 + $9/employee.
- **Strengths:** genuinely complete; hardware and software co-designed; 24/7 support; Toast IQ AI
  assistant reportedly used at 125,000+ locations by Q1 2026.
- **Weaknesses, in operators' own words:** "they charge you for everything… you sneeze and they want
  money." Mid-contract processing increases. Two-year commitment on pay-as-you-go. A $0.99 fee added
  to guest bills without operator consent, later withdrawn after backlash. Difficult exit.
- **What this teaches us:** the module-stack model maximises ARPU and poisons trust. There is a
  durable market position available to whoever refuses to play it.

#### Square for Restaurants

- **Pricing:** Free $0; Plus $49/mo per location; Premium $149/mo per location; Pro custom.
  Processing 2.6% + 15¢ (Free), 2.5% + 15¢ (Plus), 2.4% + 15¢ (Premium). KDS $30/device/mo, Kiosk
  $50/device/mo. Hardware published: $59 reader to $899 register.
- **Strengths:** the most transparent pricing among the majors; genuine free tier; branded ordering
  page on every plan including free; no contract.
- **Weaknesses:** not restaurant-only, so depth is shallower than Toast's; features live outside the
  core POS; account holds and freezes are a recurring complaint across Square's SMB base.
- **What this teaches us:** published per-location pricing with a real free tier is a proven
  acquisition engine. Copy the structure.

#### TouchBistro

- **Pricing:** POS from $69/mo; Essentials bundle $119/mo; G2 lists Dual (2 licences) $129, Team (5)
  $249, Unlimited $399. Add-ons: reservations $229/mo, loyalty $99/mo, marketing $99/mo, online
  ordering $50/mo, gift cards $25/mo, KDS.
- **Strengths:** iPad-native, hybrid local-server architecture so it keeps taking payments through a
  wifi outage — the closest incumbent to our offline thesis. No features locked out of the base POS.
- **Weaknesses:** add-ons are expensive relative to the base; reservations at $229 is more than the
  POS itself.

#### Lightspeed Restaurant

- **Pricing:** $69–399/mo depending on tier; processing 2.6–2.9% + $0.10. KDS, QR order-and-pay,
  and much else are add-ons. One register per plan.
- **Strengths:** strong inventory and demand forecasting at the top tier; multi-location and
  benchmark data on Pro.
- **Weaknesses:** costs grow with every register, location, and module; the advertised plan price is
  a poor predictor of the bill.

Three lessons from this group:

1. **The category splits into "menu" and "sales channel."** A menu should cost under $10/month.
   Anything more must be buying ordering, marketing or compliance. Be clear which you are selling.
2. **Per-table and per-order pricing is the hidden killer** and is the single most-criticised
   pricing unit in the category.
3. **Quote-only pricing is the norm at the top and is disliked.** Publishing prices is a wedge.

### Group C — adjacent categories we will be compared against

- **OpenTable:** Basic $149, Core $299, Pro $499/mo, plus roughly $1–1.50 per network cover; a
  $0.25/cover or $49/mo flat option exists. **SevenRooms:** quote-only, no cover fees, guest CRM plus
  Voice AI. SevenRooms' own data: two in five inbound restaurant calls go unanswered, and 75% of
  diners are comfortable booking with AI.
- **Owner.com:** $249/mo + 5% restaurant fee, or $499/mo flat with no per-order fee; $499 one-time
  onboarding; no contracts; branded app included. **ChowNow** from $119/mo, **Popmenu** from $179/mo.
  Their entire pitch is "stop paying 25–35% to aggregators," which is precisely the pitch that lands
  in Nigeria too.
- **Inventory specialists:** MarketMan, Craftable, ExtraChef, Supy, Cogs-Well. Table stakes are
  invoice import, recipe costing linked to live ingredient prices, variance reporting, purchase
  orders, and export to accounting.

### Group D — Africa and Nigeria

#### Orda Africa (now Moniepoint) — the one that matters

- **Product:** restaurant OS — inventory, online orders across delivery apps, analytics — plus POS
  payments, plus revenue-based working-capital lending.
- **Scale:** ~1,500 restaurants; KFC Nigeria, Eat'N'Go (Cold Stone, Pinkberry); $4m+ raised;
  acquired by Moniepoint.
- **Typical customer:** a restaurant doing ~$70,000/year, urban, busy at lunch and dinner,
  owner-operated by a couple. **Word of mouth is their number one growth driver.**
- **Revenue model:** subscription + payments take + custom integration work (including writing POS
  software for banks) + lending.
- **Strategic moves worth copying:** abandoned own-brand hardware entirely and now writes software
  for bank-issued terminals; partnered with aggregators like Glovo, who then promote Orda to their
  restaurants because an Orda restaurant is cheap for them to onboard.
- **Where they are beatable:** fintech-owned software optimises for payment volume. The guest-facing
  experience, the kitchen floor, and the compliance surface are not where a payments company invests.

#### The aggregators — Chowdeck, Glovo, Bolt Food

Chowdeck charges restaurants **15–30% commission**, and vendors routinely inflate menu prices to
absorb it. It is currently before Nigeria's Competition and Consumer Protection Tribunal over price
transparency. Consumer sentiment is visibly hostile — "I paid ₦18k for chicken and chips."

This is the single strongest commercial argument our platform can make to a Nigerian operator:
**every order that moves from an aggregator to your own table or your own channel is 15–30% of
revenue recovered.** It is Owner.com's US pitch, and the Nigerian numbers are worse, which makes the
pitch stronger.

#### Others

InkeepX (multi-currency African cloud/offline POS, free tier), Paystar Africa, JiPOS (Kenya/East
Africa), Vendy, Bumpa (SME commerce, adjacent), Oracle Simphony (enterprise, expensive, present via
hotel chains).

## 1.3 Industry standards — the bar for "not outdated"

**Must-have (absence is disqualifying):** menu and modifier management; order capture across
dine-in, takeaway and delivery; kitchen routing; payment capture and reconciliation; daily sales
reporting and cash-up; staff accounts with roles; offline tolerance; a real free trial; published
pricing; data export.

**Expected:** table management and floor plan; split bills and partial payments; discounts, comps
and voids with reasons and an audit trail; multi-location; inventory with recipe costing and
variance; shift and labour reporting; loyalty and guest CRM; branded online ordering; delivery
aggregator integration; email/SMS/WhatsApp notifications; receipt and kitchen printing; accounting
export; a public API and webhooks; role-based permissions with an audit log; mobile-responsive back
office; onboarding wizard and in-product help.

**Advanced:** demand forecasting and labour scheduling; menu engineering (margin × popularity);
automated purchase orders against par levels; anomaly detection on voids, discounts and waste;
scheduled report delivery; approval workflows; multi-currency and localisation; SSO; sandbox and
developer docs.

**Enterprise:** SSO with SAML/OIDC and SCIM provisioning; granular custom roles; immutable audit
logs with export to a SIEM; data residency; contractual SLA and priority support; consolidated
franchise reporting with per-franchisee isolation; procurement paperwork — security questionnaire,
DPA, penetration test summary, insurance certificates.

**Reporting standards:** every figure must be exportable, every currency figure must reconcile to
the payment provider's settlement, and every report must state its time zone and date basis.
Restaurants close after midnight; a report that silently uses calendar days rather than business
days is worse than no report.

## 1.4 Problems users still have

Drawn from operator forums, review sites and the competitive analysis above. Each is an opening.

### Commercial

1. **Unpredictable bills.** The advertised price is not the price. Modules, per-device fees, per-order
   fees and mid-contract rate rises make the real cost unknowable in advance.
   → *We publish a full price including everything, in naira, and never raise a rate mid-term without
   opt-out.*
2. **Lock-in.** Two-year commitments; hardware that only works with one vendor; data that is hard to
   extract.
   → *Month-to-month by default, one-click full data export, no proprietary hardware.*
3. **Aggregator commissions of 15–30%** eating the margin, with price inflation as the only defence.
   → *Own-channel ordering positioned explicitly as commission recovery, with the saving quantified
   in the dashboard.*

### Operational

4. **Manual payment reconciliation.** Someone stares at a bank app and matches transfers to tables by
   hand. It is slow, it is where theft hides, and it is unglamorous enough that nobody has fixed it.
   → *Dedicated virtual account per order; automatic matching; an exceptions queue for the rest.*
5. **Downtime from power and connectivity.** Cloud-first systems stop the restaurant.
   → *Local-first; the venue keeps trading; reconcile on reconnect.*
6. **Shrinkage.** The National Restaurant Association attributes **75% of inventory shrinkage to
   employee theft.** Voids, comps, unrecorded waste and over-portioning are the mechanisms.
   → *Every void, comp and discount attributed to a named user with a reason; theoretical-versus-actual
   variance; anomaly alerts on outlier patterns.*
7. **Data entered twice.** Menu maintained separately in the POS, on the website, and on two or three
   aggregators.
   → *One menu, published to every channel, with per-channel pricing and availability.*
8. **Slow onboarding.** Menu setup is the wall every restaurant hits. MenuTiger sells "5-minute AI
   menu setup" as its headline for a reason.
   → *Import a photo or PDF of the existing menu and produce a structured, priced, categorised menu
   for review. This is the single highest-leverage AI application in the product.*

### Experience

9. **QR ordering that feels like being abandoned.** The backlash is real and documented.
   → *Flex ordering: one ticket, writable by guest and server. Call-a-server always visible. The
   phone is never the only channel.*
10. **Poor mobile back office.** Owners are on the floor, not at a desk. Most competitors' admin is a
    desktop app that technically resizes.
    → *Owner-mode mobile app, designed for a phone: today's numbers, live orders, approve a void,
    flip a dish off.*
11. **Reports that do not answer questions.** Operators get charts, not decisions.
    → *Menu engineering, hour-of-day labour fit, and a plain-language "what changed and why" summary.*

### Administrative

12. **All-or-nothing permissions.** A manager who can see reports can usually also change prices.
13. **No audit trail**, so disputes are unresolvable.
14. **Compliance ignored.** Nigerian e-invoicing is arriving and no restaurant platform sells it.
